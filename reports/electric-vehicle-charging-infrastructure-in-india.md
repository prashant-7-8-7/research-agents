# Electric vehicle charging infrastructure in India

# Summary
India has made significant strides in expanding its electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure, with the number of public chargers increasing from around 5,000 in 2022 to approximately 39,500 by late 2025, including 8,414 fast chargers. However, despite this growth, the EV-to-charger ratio remains high, with one charger per 235 EVs, far above the global benchmark. The infrastructure is unevenly distributed, with leading states like Karnataka and Maharashtra clustering most chargers. The government has implemented several policies, including the "Go Electric" campaign and the FAME II scheme, to support the development of EV charging infrastructure. Despite these efforts, challenges such as inadequate charging stations, unreliable electricity supply, and high charging costs continue to hinder widespread adoption, particularly in smaller towns and regions.

# Key Findings

## Current State of EV Charging Infrastructure
As of 2025, India has seen a significant increase in the number of public EV chargers, from around 5,000 in 2022 to approximately 39,500, including 8,414 fast chargers. However, the EV-to-charger ratio remains high, with one charger per 235 EVs, far above the global benchmark of 1 per 6–20 [4, 5]. Public chargers have expanded rapidly, particularly in Tier-1 cities and major highways, but the infrastructure is still unevenly distributed, with leading states like Karnataka and Maharashtra clustering most chargers [4, 5].

## Government Policies Supporting EV Charging Infrastructure
The Indian government has implemented several policies to support the development of EV charging infrastructure. The Ministry of Power has clarified that charging stations do not require a license and has issued amendments to technical standards for grid connectivity and safety [7]. The Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) has been designated as the central nodal agency for promoting charging infrastructure. The government has launched the "Go Electric" campaign and prepared action plans for 9 major cities to install public charging stations [7]. Additionally, the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs has amended building by-laws to include provisions for EV charging infrastructure [7]. The Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of (Hybrid and) Electric Vehicles (FAME) II scheme has also provided substantial financial incentives for EV adoption and infrastructure development [10].

## Developed Regions for EV Charging Networks
The regions in India with the most developed electric vehicle charging networks are Karnataka and Maharashtra. Karnataka leads with 5,880 charging stations, followed by Maharashtra with 3,728 stations. These states have the largest shares of public charging stations, contributing to a more developed network compared to other regions in India [12, 13].

## Challenges in EV Charging Infrastructure Development
The development of EV charging infrastructure in India faces several challenges, including inadequate charging stations, unreliable electricity supply, and high charging costs [1, 18, 19]. These issues are particularly acute in smaller towns and regions, where the number of public charging stations is limited [5]. Additionally, the current EV-to-charger ratio in India is significantly higher than in more mature EV markets, exacerbating the problem during peak travel seasons [5]. The uneven distribution of chargers, with a concentration in Tier-1 cities and major highways, also hinders widespread adoption [5]. To address these challenges, improvements in infrastructure, grid reliability, and renewable energy integration are necessary [1, 18, 19].

## Incentives and Subsidies under FAME II Scheme
Under the FAME II scheme, the Government of India provided incentives for the development of electric vehicle charging infrastructure through the installation of charging stations. Specifically, 2,877 electric vehicle charging stations were sanctioned in 68 cities across 25 States/UTs, and 1,576 charging stations were installed across 9 expressways and 16 highways [23]. These incentives were part of the overall ₹10,000 crore budget allocated to FAME II for a period of three years starting from April 1, 2019 [20, 22].

## Impact on EV Adoption Rates
The expansion of electric vehicle charging infrastructure in India has had a positive impact on the adoption rates of electric vehicles, particularly in regions with more developed networks [26, 27]. However, despite a tenfold increase in charging stations, user experience issues and high costs continue to hinder electric vehicle adoption [27]. Furthermore, regional variations in EV adoption rates exist, with the pace of adoption remaining uneven across states and union territories [28]. These disparities suggest that while infrastructure development is crucial, additional efforts are needed to address cost and user experience issues to accelerate EV adoption across the country.

# Open Questions

2. What strategies can be employed to improve the reliability of the electricity supply for EV charging stations in smaller towns and regions?
3. How can the uneven distribution of chargers be addressed to ensure more widespread adoption of electric vehicles in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities?
