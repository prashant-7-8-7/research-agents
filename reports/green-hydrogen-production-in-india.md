# Green hydrogen production in India

# Summary
The National Green Hydrogen Mission (NGHM) in India aims to position the country as a global leader in green hydrogen production by leveraging its abundant renewable energy sources. The mission includes various policy provisions, such as waiving Interstate transmission charges and facilitating renewable energy banking, and targets 125 GW of new renewable energy capacity by 2030. Challenges include high capital expenditures for electrolyzers, gaps in transportation and storage technologies, and material dependencies. The potential applications of green hydrogen include decarbonizing hard-to-abate sectors like transportation and shipping, replacing traditional fossil fuels, and contributing to energy security. Specific technologies for transportation and storage include compressed gaseous hydrogen, liquid hydrogen, and metal hydrides. The government plans to address high production costs through subsidies for electrolyzer manufacturing and performance-based production incentives.

# Key Findings

## Current Methods of Green Hydrogen Production
The current methods of green hydrogen production in India include the National Green Hydrogen Mission (NGHM), which aims to build the necessary capacity and ecosystem to position India as a global leader in clean hydrogen [2, 6]. By 2030, the mission plans to support about 125 GW of new renewable energy capacity dedicated to green hydrogen production, along with investments exceeding ₹8 lakh crore [2]. This initiative is expected to create over 6 lakh jobs and contribute to India's energy security and self-reliance goals [3]. India is also leveraging its abundant renewable energy sources, particularly solar and wind, to drive down the cost of green hydrogen production, aiming for cost-efficient production by 2050 [1, 6].

## Government Policies Supporting Green Hydrogen Production
The government of India supports green hydrogen production through the National Green Hydrogen Mission (NGHM), which includes several policy provisions [7, 2, 11]. These provisions include waiving Interstate transmission charges for renewable energy used in green hydrogen production, facilitating renewable energy banking, and ensuring time-bound grant of Open Access and connectivity for green hydrogen projects. Additionally, the mission aims to develop supply chains for hydrogen transportation and distribution, coordinate regulations and standards, facilitate research and development, and implement skill development programs [7, 2]. The mission also covers public awareness, stakeholder outreach, and international cooperation [7]. As of May 2025, 19 companies have been allocated a cumulative annual production capacity of 862,000 tonnes of green hydrogen, and 15 firms have been awarded a 3,000 MW annual electrolyzer manufacturing capacity [9].

## Challenges in Scaling Up Green Hydrogen Production
The challenges in scaling up green hydrogen production in India include high capital expenditures for electrolyzers, gaps in transportation and storage technologies, and material dependencies [13]. Additionally, renewable power and electrolyser hardware remain major cost drivers, with limited scope for dramatic price drops before 2030 [15]. Infrastructure, water scarcity, and supply chain risks also pose substantial scaling barriers [15]. As of late 2025, the true cost of green hydrogen production in India remains in the $3.7–6/kg range, significantly higher than the $2/kg target by 2030 [15].

## Potential Applications of Green Hydrogen
Green hydrogen has emerged as a key player in India's transition to cleaner energy, with potential applications across various sectors. It can decarbonize hard-to-abate sectors such as transportation, shipping, and steel [2]. Green hydrogen can also replace traditional fossil fuels in transportation and industry, contributing to energy security and reducing carbon emissions [16]. Additionally, it can be used in fuel cells to power vehicles, provide electricity, and in heating systems, making it a versatile energy source [16]. India has launched the National Green Hydrogen Mission to support the development of a green hydrogen economy, aiming to achieve energy independence and reduce import dependence on fossil fuels [7].

## Technologies for Transportation and Storage of Green Hydrogen
Specific technologies for the transportation and storage of green hydrogen in India include compressed gaseous hydrogen storage, liquid hydrogen storage, and metal hydrides or chemical carriers [20]. Compressed gaseous hydrogen storage, typically between 200 bar and 700 bar, is noted for its technical maturity and fast deployment, although it requires more land for larger storage volumes and expands safety zones at higher pressures [20]. Liquid hydrogen storage, which requires cryogenic cooling below −253°C, is energy-intensive and costly, limiting its near-term adoption [20]. The document also highlights that India is developing hydrogen technologies for the transport sector, particularly for heavy-duty trucking, where fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs) or hydrogen internal combustion engines (H2-ICE) could play a significant role [21].

## Subsidies and Financial Mechanisms to Address High Costs
The Indian government has launched the National Green Hydrogen Mission (NGHM) in 2023 to address the high costs of green hydrogen production [2]. According to the mission, subsidies will be provided through two key channels: upfront capital subsidies for electrolyzer manufacturing capacity and performance-based production incentives tied to the quantity of green hydrogen generated [23]. Additionally, the mission aims to drive down production costs through financial incentives for electrolyser manufacturing and green hydrogen production, and to boost domestic demand by mandating minimum green hydrogen consumption in specific sectors [3].
